In Sections 6, 7 we describe all stable pairs parametrized by the boundary of F¯En,2subscript¯𝐹En2{\overline{F}}_{\rm En,2}. The irreducible components of these pairs turn out to be surfaces that naturally correspond to the ABCDE Dynkin diagrams. They generalize the ADE surfaces of [AT21] that appear on the boundary of K3 moduli spaces.

In Section 4 we also provide a detailed description of some nice models of Enriques degenerations, which are slightly more singular than simple normal crossing. Instead, they are dlt (divisorially log terminal, see [KM98, Def. 2.37]).

The validity of our description of these Kulikov, dlt, and stable models relies on the general theory of compactifications of moduli spaces of K3 surfaces developed by the first two authors [AE23, AE22]. Most relevant to the situation at hand, [AE22] considers the 757575 moduli spaces of K3 surfaces with a non-symplectic involution. For 505050 of them, the ramification divisor R𝑅R contains a component C𝐶C of genus g​(C)≥2𝑔𝐶2g(C)\geq 2 providing a polarization. For these, [AE22] describes the Kulikov models and KSBA compactification for the pairs (X,ϵ​C)𝑋italic-ϵ𝐶(X,\epsilon C).

Crucial for us here is the compactified moduli space F¯(2,2,0)subscript¯𝐹220{\overline{F}}_{(2,2,0)} of K3 surfaces of degree 444 with a del Pezzo involution ιdPsubscript𝜄dP\iota_{{\rm dP}} corresponding, generically, to double covers of ℙ1×ℙ1superscriptℙ1superscriptℙ1{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1} branched over a curve of bidegree (4,4)44(4,4). By immersing the moduli space F¯En,2→F¯(2,2,0)→subscript¯𝐹En2subscript¯𝐹220{\overline{F}}_{\rm En,2}\to{\overline{F}}_{(2,2,0)} and understanding the Enriques involution on the fibers, we give a description of F¯En,2subscript¯𝐹En2{\overline{F}}_{\rm En,2} and its universal family.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we discuss the model of Enriques surfaces which we use in this paper. We also recall the description of the moduli space FEn,2subscript𝐹En2F_{\rm En,2} after Sterk [Ste91]. Then we describe morphisms between FEn,2subscript𝐹En2F_{\rm En,2}, F(2,2,0)subscript𝐹220F_{(2,2,0)} and the moduli F(10,10,0)subscript𝐹10100F_{(10,10,0)} of unpolarized Enriques surfaces. Finally, we briefly recall the theory of KSBA stable pairs and their compact moduli.

In Section 3 we recall the cusp diagrams of F(2,2,0)subscript𝐹220F_{(2,2,0)}, F(10,10,0)subscript𝐹10100F_{(10,10,0)}, FEn,2subscript𝐹En2F_{\rm En,2} and determine how they map to each other. Next, we describe Coxeter diagrams associated to each of the five 00-cusps and the 999 111-cusps of FEn,2subscript𝐹En2F_{\rm En,2}. The diagrams are the same as in Sterk [Ste91] but we find them in a different way, “folding by involutions” the Coxeter diagrams of the lattices U⊕E82direct-sum𝑈superscriptsubscript𝐸82U\oplus E_{8}^{2} and U​(2)⊕E82direct-sum𝑈2superscriptsubscript𝐸82U(2)\oplus E_{8}^{2} corresponding to the two 00-cusps of F(2,2,0)subscript𝐹220F_{(2,2,0)}. This is the combinatorial heart of the paper. An idea from [AET23, AE22] employed here is that one can read off degenerations of K3 surfaces directly from Coxeter diagrams. Consequently, we are able to read off degenerations of Enriques surfaces from the Coxeter diagrams of FEn,2subscript𝐹En2F_{\rm En,2}.

Using the above description, in Section 4 we find integral-affine spheres with two commuting involutions ιEn,IAsubscript𝜄EnIA\iota_{{\rm En},\,{\rm IA}} and ιdP,IAsubscript𝜄dPIA\iota_{{\rm dP},{\rm IA}} and the corresponding Kulikov models of K3 surfaces with involutions. We then describe how to construct the dlt models for degenerations of Enriques surfaces, and give some detailed examples.

In Section 5, as an application of general theory and in a similar way to [ABE22, AE22] we describe the KSBA compactification F¯En,2subscript¯𝐹En2{\overline{F}}_{\rm En,2} and the stable pairs appearing on the boundary. For K3 surfaces, the irreducible components of degenerations are ADE surfaces of [AT21]. In the case of Enriques surfaces, additional B and C surfaces appear, corresponding to B𝐵B and C𝐶C Dynkin diagrams resulting from folding ADE diagrams. We describe them in Sections 6 and 7.

We work over the complex numbers, although most of the results can be generalized to any field of characteristic ≠2absent2\neq 2.
